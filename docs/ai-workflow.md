# AI-assisted engineering workflow

AI was used intentionally as an accelerator, not as a source of truth.

| Activity | Assistance | Human verification |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Extracted assessment themes and challenged scope assumptions | Clarifying questions were sent to the company; their answers became explicit defaults and exclusions. |
| Architecture | Compared stack, persistence, currency, and deployment options | Selected choices were checked against the Java/Angular role, assessment size, free-tier constraints, and maintainability. |
| Implementation | Assisted with scaffolding, repetitive API/UI code, and test ideas | Code was compiled, tests were run, schemas and transactions were inspected, and failures were corrected. |
| Review | Suggested edge cases and interview questions | Only claims supported by implemented behavior and verification are retained. |

Rejected ideas include microservices, live FX, total compensation, generative natural-language queries, and full enterprise RBAC. They increased complexity or risk without advancing the confirmed core problem.

Representative prompts asked for: a requirements-first plan; effective-dated salary invariants; deterministic multi-country seed data; safe CSV failure semantics; curated analytics; free Render deployment constraints; and focused test cases. No real employee, salary, credential, or company-confidential data was supplied to AI.
